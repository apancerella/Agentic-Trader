# Weekly Scan for New Candidates

**Purpose.** Surface new swing-trade candidates that fit the strategy, so the watchlist doesn't go stale — both confirmed setups (reactive scans) and names showing early positioning ahead of a move (leading-indicator scans).

**Schedule.** Weekly, Sunday 6:00 PM ET (ahead of Monday's earnings-watch pass and the trading week). Linked Routine: "Weekly Scan (Sun 6pm ET)" (`trig_013fd83SVdupRpgnAjPwAPCr`), self-bound to the session at `session_01WAYdETVewPXXJGZ9qTaTAa`. Cron `0 22 * * 0` (UTC) — correct for 6 PM ET while EDT is in effect; needs updating to `0 23 * * 0` when clocks fall back to EST in November.

**Steps.**
1. `get_scans` to see existing saved scans; `run_scan` on one that fits swing-trade criteria (liquid, trending/breakout, reasonable volume), or `create_scan` if none exists yet. Drop any hit with an empty `market_cap` field first — the mechanical signature of leveraged/inverse ETFs, ETNs, and basket/trust products; note the count skipped.
2. **Proactive catalyst research**, independent of the scan: check for upcoming catalysts (FDA decisions, index rebalances, M&A chatter, major conferences, relevant macro releases) for current watchlist symbols and their sectors via `get_earnings_calendar`/`search`/`get_equity_fundamentals`, even if nothing has moved yet — this is what `opportunity-scan` reads back to connect a later price move to a known reason.
3. **Momentum-building alert.** The breakout scan in step 1 only ever catches a name *after* it's already up 3%+ in a single day — real accumulation phases (steady RSI climb, rising volume, no single day dramatic enough to trip that filter) sail through undetected until the move is already extended. Catch that earlier:
   - Run the looser "Weekly Scan — Early Momentum" saved scan (same $10 price / 500k avg-volume liquidity floor, but %-change > 1.5% and relative volume > 1.15 instead of 3% / 1.3x) and apply the same empty-`market_cap` junk filter. This bar is noisy by design — expect the large majority of hits to be junk (leveraged/inverse ETFs and crypto-wrapper products dominate a 1.5% day almost every session); the junk filter is what makes it usable at all.
   - For every survivor, plus every current `memory/watchlist.md` symbol regardless of today's move, pull RSI over the trailing 2–3 weeks (`get_equity_technical_indicators`, daily). Flag a name as **building momentum** only when *all* of: RSI has been climbing across at least 3 consecutive sessions (a rising trajectory, not one spike), RSI now sits in the 55–65 band (past neutral, not yet the ≥65 line this repo treats as extended), and the cumulative price return over the trailing 5–10 sessions is meaningfully positive (roughly ≥5%) despite no single day having cleared the 3% breakout-scan bar.
   - Cross-check `get_earnings_calendar` for anything flagged this way — a rising-RSI trend running straight into a print in the next 7 days is a different, riskier situation (anticipation risk) than one with a clear runway, and should be called out explicitly rather than folded into the same flag.
   - This is a "watch closer" signal, not a proposal trigger — journal it even when nothing else about the name is actionable yet, so `opportunity-scan` can read it back later and recognize a subsequent breakout as something already on watch rather than something out of nowhere.
4. **Leading-signal scans — positioning ahead of a move.** Steps 1 and 3 are both reactive at their core (step 3 catches accumulation earlier, but still after RSI and price have started moving). This step needs neither: run **"Predictive Scan — Unusual Options Activity"** (relative options volume > 4x normal on a $1B+ name still under a 3% move — note the call/put skew from the `Total call volume`/`Total put volume` columns, since a lopsided ratio on real volume is informed positioning ahead of a move) and **"Predictive Scan — Stealth Accumulation"** (relative stock volume > 1.5x normal while price is still flat, -1% to +2%). Same junk filter as step 1. Fold survivors into the candidate set for steps 6-7 below with a "leading, unconfirmed" label — they get the same fundamentals/technicals workup as any other candidate before reaching step 8.
5. **Sector relative-strength check:** pull performance over the last week and month for a fixed sector-ETF basket (XLK, XLF, XLE, XLV, XLY, XLP, XLI, XLB, XLU, XLRE, XLC), rank them, and journal the ranking to catch rotations proactively. Cross-check any step-4 leading-signal survivor against this ranking — the same signal in a top-ranked sector is a materially stronger combined read than in a lagging one; call it out when it lines up.
6. For each surviving scan hit — reactive and leading alike — pull fundamentals (`get_equity_fundamentals`) and technicals (`get_equity_technical_indicators`, `get_equity_historicals`, including SMA 50/200 trend context and the nearest `pivot_points` level) to sanity-check the setup.
7. Cross-check against `memory/watchlist.md` to avoid duplicates and against open positions to respect the max-7-concurrent-positions guardrail.
8. Propose watchlist additions with a one-line thesis each — this routine proposes watchlist changes, not trades. Adding to the watchlist still requires user confirmation before `memory/watchlist.md` is updated (per `CLAUDE.md`); on approval, capture the current price via `get_equity_quotes` for the new "Price at Add" column.

**Guardrails.** Never places a trade. Respect the max-7-new-proposals/week guardrail when the *next* step (an actual trade proposal) is made from a watchlist entry. The momentum-building alert and the step-4 leading-signal scans never add to the watchlist or propose a trade on their own — they're journaled observations that still need the usual sanity-check and thesis before a proposal.

**Journal output.** Append to `memory/journal/<date>.md`: scans used + criteria (reactive and leading), hits skipped as junk across all scans, upcoming catalysts found, names flagged as building momentum (with the RSI trend, cumulative move, and earnings-proximity read for each), any leading-signal survivor (options skew or relative-volume read, and whether it lines up with a top-ranked sector), the sector relative-strength ranking, candidates found (with trend/pivot context), proposed watchlist additions and thesis, user's decisions on each.

**Dashboard output.** After journaling (and merging any watchlist changes), rebuild and republish the "Agentic Trading Desk" Artifact (see `.claude/skills/trading-dashboard/`) so it reflects this run.
